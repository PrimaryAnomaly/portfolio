## Context

Self-driving laboratories hand protocol execution to robotic platforms and, increasingly, to AI agents that plan experiments and adjust protocols as results come in. That combination creates accident and misuse pathways that neither conventional lab safety nor conventional software security covers on its own.

This Opinion paper, "Securing self-driving labs against accident and misuse", is written for Trends in Biotechnology. It treats the terrestrial self-driving lab as one system and asks where its controls have to live.

## Scope

- **Cybersecurity.** Networked instruments, cloud schedulers and remote access widen the attack surface of a lab that can act on the physical world.
- **Biosafety and biosecurity.** Automated synthesis and culture work can produce harm through error as well as intent, and the two need different controls.
- **Agent-mediated protocol control.** When an AI agent can rewrite a protocol mid-run, review and authorisation have to happen inside the loop, not after it.

## Status

The manuscript is active and in preparation. Later phases, an orbital companion piece and a full-length paper on autonomous biolabs in orbit, are parked until this one is submitted.
